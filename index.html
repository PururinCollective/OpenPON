Open PON Foundation

Opening the future of
network connectivity

Passive optical networks are the last mile for hundreds of millions of subscribers, yet the equipment at the end of that fibre has long been closed, locked and disposable. We work to change that — with open firmware, cross-vendor interoperability and the simple principle that the device in your home should be yours.

Mission

An open last mile, built on principles that already have a track record

Open PON Foundation is a volunteer, vendor-neutral effort. Our vision aligns with three organisations that have already proven these ideas at scale.

Electronic Frontier Foundation

Aligned vision: user rights & privacy

The right to understand, repair and control the hardware you paid for — and to keep what happens on your connection private.

Open Networking Foundation

Aligned vision: open, disaggregated networks

Open PON standards and interoperable, software-defined access, rather than a network defined end to end by a single vendor.

Linux Foundation

Aligned vision: open collaboration

Neutral governance, shared engineering effort and open source as the practical way to build infrastructure that outlives any one company.

Cross-vendor compatibility

One stick that speaks to many OLTs. Compatibility work sits at the core of every firmware project we support, because interoperability is what makes choice real.

Ease of access

Clear guides, reachable regional suppliers and tooling that does not demand a lab bench. Knowledge that stays locked inside private forums helps no one.

User-owned equipment

Your ONT is part of your network, not an unremovable extension of the operator’s. Bring your own device, keep your own router, replace it when you choose.

Privacy

No vendor cloud, no silent telemetry. Where an operator mandates TR-069, the firmware emulates it — management gets its answer, and nothing personal leaves your network.

A word on responsibility

Open PON Foundation discourages reckless DIY deployment. PON is a shared medium — one misbehaving ONU affects every subscriber on the split.

Safety Notice

Custom GPON SFP ONTs must be handled with extreme caution

Custom GPON SFP ONTs operate outside standard ISP-controlled environments. Improper configuration or non-compliant behaviour can destabilise the optical network. A single faulty ONU may trigger a PON-wide disruption, impacting multiple subscribers on the same fibre split. Network operators actively monitor such anomalies and may enforce service suspension, account bans, or financial penalties.

Operational risks

PON blackout events

Misbehaving ONTs can disrupt upstream synchronisation, affecting all users on the same GPON segment.

ISP enforcement actions

Unauthorised devices or configurations may result in immediate disconnection, blacklisting, or penalties.

Unpredictable stability

Custom firmware and configurations may lead to intermittent connectivity or complete service failure.

Responsibility & support

This project promotes open knowledge and interoperability, not reckless deployment.

All experimentation is performed at your own risk. Contributors may provide guidance when available, but support is voluntary, limited, and not guaranteed.

  • Do not endanger shared infrastructure.
  • Do not disrupt others.
  • Proceed with discipline.

A live network is not a place to experiment. Have your deployment planned, installed and tested by a professional — that is what our regional integrators are for.
